EDRONE · SELF-SERVICE & PRODUCT EDUCATION~300 articles · ~2 months · Brazil + Poland

Turning a Help Center into a scalable self-service experience.

During a major product evolution, I became the Brazilian focal point for restructuring and standardizing a large Help Center — treating documentation as part of the customer experience, not just a writing task.

Product EducationSelf-ServiceContent UXInformation Architecture
THE SHIFT
FromOrganic, inconsistent documentation
↓
ToClearer, searchable, governed self-service content
Read case study +

Context

The product was going through a significant redesign and new-feature cycle. Meanwhile, Help Center content had grown organically across multiple contributors, creating inconsistencies in tone, terminology, structure, formatting, and organization.

Challenge

The task was bigger than updating articles. Customers needed to find reliable answers while the product itself was changing, and the content system needed enough consistency to remain maintainable after the transformation.

My role

I was the Brazilian focal point for the transformation. I reviewed and updated approximately 300 articles in roughly two months, aligned terminology and content patterns, supported other contributors, and collaborated across Brazil and Poland.

Key insight

Documentation only works as self-service when it matches how customers look for help. Clarity was not enough; discoverability mattered too.

Approach

  1. AuditIdentify inconsistencies created by organic growth and product changes.
  2. StandardizeCreate repeatable structures, terminology, and writing patterns.
  3. Design for searchConsider customer questions, keywords, and how support conversations were phrased.
  4. ScaleGuide additional contributors and align tutorial conventions across markets.

Design decisions

Search behavior over internal vocabulary

Content was structured with the words customers were likely to use when looking for an answer.

Patterns over one-off fixes

Standardization made the content easier to scan and gave future contributors a clearer model to follow.

One experience across formats

Tutorial videos were also brought closer to a shared visual and instructional standard.

Impact

~300articles reviewed / updated
~2 monthsdelivery window
2 marketsBrazil + Poland alignment

No verified product-adoption or support-deflection metric is available, so this case does not claim one.

What I learned

Self-service content is part of the product experience: if users cannot find, understand, and act on an answer, the documentation has not finished its job.

Public context

The Help Center has continued to evolve since this project. The current public environment is linked only as context and should not be interpreted as a claim that its present-day state is entirely my work.

CONCENTRIX · PRODUCT ONBOARDINGRole-based · Interactive · SCORM

Designing role-based onboarding for a digital platform.

Instead of teaching every feature to every user, I helped design an onboarding experience around what different roles actually needed to do — combining guided learning with interactive product practice.

Product AdoptionOnboardingRole-Based LearningSimulation
User role→Task→Learning need→Practice→Action
Read case study +

Challenge

A new platform would be used by regular users, managers, and administrators. A generic walkthrough would force people to learn functionality that was irrelevant to their responsibilities.

Key insight

The product menu was not the learning architecture. The right structure was role → task → need → action.

My role

I worked on the learning architecture and production of a blended interactive onboarding experience using product recordings, guided explanation, role-based paths, interactive simulation, and SCORM delivery.

Solution

The experience combined guided explanations with moments where the learner had to reproduce clicks and actions from the product interface. An AI avatar supported the narrative, then stepped back when practice mattered more than explanation.

Design decisions

Role-based paths

Users focused on responsibilities and tasks relevant to their role instead of navigating an exhaustive feature list.

Practice over passive watching

Interactive simulations turned product education into guided action.

Technology in service of behavior

Storyline, Synthesia, screen recordings, and SCORM were implementation choices — not the learning strategy itself.

Impact

The delivered experience combined role-based learning, guided product education, and interactive practice. No verified usage, completion, or adoption metric is available, so the case focuses on the design decisions and delivered experience rather than claiming behavioral impact.

What I learned

Product onboarding becomes more useful when it is organized around what users need to accomplish, not around how the product happens to be organized.

GRUPO FLEURY · BEHAVIOR CHANGEHealthcare · 200+ employees · 2-month challenge

Designing a learning campaign to turn awareness into behavior change.

A broad privacy-education brief became a measurable, two-month behavior-change campaign combining storytelling, weekly communication, and repeated observation.

Learning StrategyBehavior ChangeMeasurementHealthcare
DESIGN LOGIC
Baseline → Education → Weekly reinforcement → Measurement
Read case study +

Context

As Brazil's data-protection law was being introduced, a healthcare HR environment handled large volumes of sensitive employee information and printed documents. The brief was broad: educate employees and encourage better handling of sensitive information.

Challenge

Awareness alone would not show whether behavior had changed. The initiative needed a way to make an abstract compliance topic concrete, memorable, and measurable.

Key insight

If the goal is behavior change, the experience needs more than a one-time explanation. It needs reinforcement and observable signals.

My role

I designed the learning and engagement campaign, created the animated narrative and supporting presentation, established a baseline, launched the initiative to 200+ employees, measured the same indicators weekly, and created the ongoing communication.

Approach

  1. Define observable behaviorMeasure printing volume, sensitive information in the sample, and documents left near the printing area.
  2. Make the topic understandableUse characters and storytelling to introduce privacy, sensitive data, and responsible document handling.
  3. Keep attention over timePublish weekly progress communication plus a serialized mystery/comic narrative.
  4. Measure repeatedlyReturn every Friday for two months and track the same indicators.

Impact

The project generated measurable changes in monitored behaviors and was selected as a Top 10 finalist in an internal innovation and resource-optimization recognition program. Exact reduction percentages are intentionally omitted here until the original measurement materials are fully validated.

What I learned

Learning is more powerful when the desired behavior is defined before the content — and when communication continues long enough to reinforce the change.
