Concentrix · Product Onboarding

Designing role-based onboarding for a digital platform.

Instead of teaching every feature to every user, I helped design an onboarding experience around what different roles actually needed to do — combining guided learning with interactive product practice.

Product AdoptionRole-Based LearningInteractive SimulationSCORM

The challenge

A new platform would be used by regular users, managers, and administrators. A generic walkthrough would force people to learn functionality that was irrelevant to their responsibilities.

My role

I worked on the learning architecture and production of a blended interactive onboarding experience using product recordings, guided explanation, role-based paths, interactive simulation, and SCORM delivery.

The insight

The product menu was not the learning architecture. The right structure was role → task → need → action.

The solution

The experience combined guided explanations with moments where learners had to reproduce clicks and actions from the product interface. An AI avatar supported the narrative, then stepped back when practice mattered more than explanation.

01 · RoleStart with the user's responsibilities.
02 · TaskIdentify what that person actually needs to accomplish.
03 · PracticeGuide the learner through realistic product interactions.
04 · ActionMove from explanation to doing.

Design decisions

Role-based paths

Users focused on tasks relevant to their role instead of navigating an exhaustive feature list.

Practice over passive watching

Interactive simulations turned product education into guided action.

Technology in service of behavior

Storyline, Synthesia, recordings, and SCORM were implementation choices — not the strategy itself.

Impact

The delivered experience combined role-based learning, guided product education, and interactive practice.

No verified usage, completion, or adoption metric is available. The case therefore focuses on the design decisions and delivered experience rather than claiming behavioral impact.

What I learned

Product onboarding becomes more useful when it is organized around what users need to accomplish, not around how the product happens to be organized.
